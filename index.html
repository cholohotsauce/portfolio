Ace Cashout · case study

From a tip calculator to a payroll tool

The Ace is a restaurant where the floor staff split tips at the end of every night. This started as a one-screen calculator for that split and grew into the system the owner uses to pay tips each week.

The problem

At close, someone adds the cash and card tips, takes the kitchen’s share and divides the rest by hours worked. On a phone calculator that was slow and easy to get wrong by a few cents.

The bigger cost sat with the owner. Every week he opened a folder of nightly PDFs, one by one, and added everything up by hand.

What I built

For the closing shift

Enter the tips, tap who worked, and the split appears live. One tap saves the night. It installs to the iPad and still works with no signal, then uploads when the connection is back.

For the owner

A Payday view that opens on the right week, a Thursday email with who to pay, pay stubs for each staff member, trends, and a change log nobody can edit.

Decisions worth explaining

Money in whole cents

Shares are worked out in cents with largest-remainder rounding, so payouts plus the kitchen’s share always equal the tips. Unit tests pin it down.

Paid weeks are locked

A save takes a lock on that week’s row in the database. Once a week is paid, nothing can change money that already went out.

Emails are claimed once

Each email first claims a row in a log, so a retried job never sends a duplicate pay stub.

Offline without a sync engine

A service worker caches the calculator, unsent saves wait on the device, and the server treats a save as an upsert.

Outcome

Paying tips went from opening every PDF and adding them up by hand to reading one email and tapping one button, and every number has a trail behind it.
